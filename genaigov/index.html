Why this matters now

GenAI is no longer a classroom-only issue.

It now touches teaching and learning, assessment and integrity, research ethics, student equity, data privacy, and public trust — yet most institutional responses still address one domain at a time.

The issue is not whether universities are responding to GenAI. It is whether they can govern it coherently across the institution.
— Core thesis, cross-regional governance research
Six policy domains

What the assessment covers.

Each domain is reviewed through 7–8 items rated on a four-level maturity scale (Not in place · Emerging · Established · Embedded). A separate coherence module diagnoses how well your policies connect.

i.

Teaching & Learning

Curriculum design, pedagogy, AI literacy, and staff capability for teaching with GenAI.

ii.

Assessment & Integrity

Assessment design, declaration of GenAI use, misconduct procedures, and assessor capability.

iii.

Research Ethics

HREC processes, authorship, research methods guidance, and integrity training.

iv.

Equity & Access

Equitable access, accessibility, support for diverse cohorts, and Indigenous considerations.

v.

Data Privacy

Approved tools, data classification, procurement review, and cybersecurity assessment.

vi.

Public Trust

Transparency, accountable decision-making, stakeholder voice, and public reporting.
