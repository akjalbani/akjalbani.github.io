Methodology & provenance

How the framework was built.

GAIA-HE is a diagnostic instrument grounded in comparative cross-regional research on how universities in Asia, Europe, and Australia are responding to Generative AI.

Across the three regions, universities have produced an active and growing set of policies, statements, and guidance on Generative AI. However, this activity often remains fragmented — distributed across separate units, written for different audiences, and operating on different timelines. The result is policy activity without policy coherence.

GAIA-HE was designed to help institutions diagnose this fragmentation and identify a pathway toward coherent, whole-of-institution governance. It does not score one institution against another; it offers a structured mirror against which an institution can examine itself.

The six domains

Six policy domains were identified through review of recent cross-regional scholarship on institutional responses to GenAI. They represent the principal areas in which universities have been producing guidance and where governance gaps most commonly emerge:

  • Teaching & Learning — pedagogy, curriculum design, AI literacy, staff capability.
  • Assessment & Academic Integrity — declaration, design, detection, misconduct procedures.
  • Research Ethics — ethics approval, authorship, research methods, integrity training.
  • Equity & Access — equitable access, accessibility, support for diverse cohorts.
  • Data Privacy & Transparency — approved tools, data classification, procurement, security.
  • Public Trust — transparency, accountable decision-making, stakeholder voice, public reporting.

The coherence module

Alongside the six domain assessments, a separate eight-item coherence module captures whether policies connect — whether they cross-reference each other, share terminology, have a named accountable owner, operate under a defined review cycle, and form part of a coherent institutional strategy. The coherence score is reported separately because high domain maturity does not guarantee institutional coherence, and an institution may have strong individual policies that nonetheless operate in isolation.

Scoring

Each domain item is rated on a four-level maturity scale: Not in place (0), Emerging (1), Established (2), Embedded (3). A domain score is the proportion of the maximum achieved (0–100). The overall maturity score is the average of the six domain scores. The coherence score is computed similarly from the eight coherence items, on a three-level scale (No, Partial, Yes).

Five maturity tiers express overall score bands: Tier 1 Reactive (0–20), Tier 2 Emerging (21–40), Tier 3 Developing (41–60), Tier 4 Coherent (61–80), and Tier 5 Leading (81–100).

How the instrument should be used

GAIA-HE is a diagnostic, not a compliance audit. It is most useful when:

  • Completed collaboratively by an institutional working group rather than by one individual.
  • Used to surface and discuss disagreement about where the institution currently sits.
  • Repeated over time to track movement, rather than treated as a one-off measure.
  • Combined with qualitative evidence — policy documents, committee minutes, staff and student feedback.

Scores should be read as an invitation to inquiry, not as a final verdict. Two institutions with the same overall score may differ substantially in where their strengths and gaps lie, and in the appropriate next steps.

Privacy and data handling

GAIA-HE runs entirely in your browser. All responses are stored in your device's local storage. No data is sent to a server, no analytics are collected, and no institutional information leaves your device. To clear your responses at any time, use the Reset assessment button on the results page.

Limitations

GAIA-HE is a self-assessment tool. Like all self-assessments, it is susceptible to optimism bias and to differences in interpretation between respondents. It is not a substitute for formal external review or audit. The recommendations generated are general in nature and should be adapted to the institution's specific governance context, regulatory environment, and strategic priorities.

The instrument is also a snapshot in time. The GenAI policy landscape is evolving rapidly, and the questions and tier descriptors will need periodic revision. Comments and suggestions on the instrument are welcomed.
